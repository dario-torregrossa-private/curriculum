## Project overview

**EDWARDS** — *Energy optimisation of WAstewateR treatment plants through KPI analysis and Decision Support Systems* — was a research project led by the Luxembourg Institute of Science and Technology (LIST), in partnership with the University of Luxembourg and the University of Valencia. The project was funded by the Luxembourg National Research Fund (FNR).

EDWARDS followed the Interreg IVB project **INNERS** (*Innovative Energy Recovery Strategies in the urban water cycle*), which ended in 2015. The projects addressed the energy intensity of wastewater treatment plants, with particular attention to the biological treatment stages, which account for a significant share of plant energy consumption.

## Shared Knowledge Decision Support System

The project resulted in the development of the **Shared Knowledge Decision Support System (SK-DSS)**, a decision support system designed to help wastewater treatment plant operators reduce energy consumption.

SK-DSS combines:

- Key performance indicators
- Fuzzy logic
- Expert knowledge
- Artificial intelligence
- High-frequency sensor and process data

The system analyses plant processes and provides case-specific suggestions to operators. Unlike plant-specific decision-support tools based mainly on low-frequency aggregated data, SK-DSS was designed to be plant-generic and to support the analysis of multiple wastewater treatment plants. It also provides a platform for plant managers to exchange information and cooperate.

## Research contribution

As part of EDWARDS, I carried out the doctoral research *A decision support system for energy saving in Waste Water Treatment Plants* at LIST, under the supervision of researchers from the University of Luxembourg, LIST, RTC4Water, and the University of Valencia.

The research proposed the SK-DSS model and its web application, together with applications targeting the energy performance of:

- Pumps
- Blowers
- Biogas systems

The project demonstrated how key performance indicators, domain expertise, data analysis, and artificial intelligence can be combined to support more efficient management of complex wastewater treatment processes.
